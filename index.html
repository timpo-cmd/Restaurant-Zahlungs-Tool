Made by Google 2025

For the 2025 edition of Made by Google, we helped reimagine the brand’s annual hardware launch, turning the traditional keynote into a lively, late-night-style show in New York City hosted by Jimmy Fallon.

My role focused on the overall guest experience and spatial design, shaping a seamless journey from arrival to stage. The environment drew from an on-set aesthetic, blending backstage utility with Google’s brand language to create a space that felt both elevated and intentional.

From the custom chrome welcome desk to the wayfinding and wall graphics, every design element reflected Google’s material sensibility and commitment to clarity through form. The palette merged industrial finishes and tactile details, balancing the precision of production design with the warmth of human touch.

This collaboration continued our partnership with Google, reinforcing a shared ambition to transform product launches into cultural experiences that connect technology, creativity, and human storytelling.

1/9

Google
CES 2024

At CES 2024, we played a pivotal role in showcasing Google's Android mobile operating system by creating a unique 18,000-square-foot interactive space.


This space was highlighted by an impressive 80-foot canopy and featured 15 analog-inspired interactive stations, designed to allow attendees to explore Android’s latest features in an environment that reflected Google’s open and connected philosophy.


Moving away from Google's typical minimalist presence, we adopted a more engaging "choose your own adventure" style, transforming the exhibit into a modern-day "Visitor Attraction Center."


Here, attendees could engage with the technologies that interested them the most. Additionally, we implemented skeuomorphic design elements to make the innovative features approachable, exemplified by a postcard-making station modeled after a mail collection box, which helped make the technology both accessible and familiar to users.

1/26

Sonos

Newsa

I had the opportunity to work for several years as a creative director for Sonos, overseeing the launch of the new collection of audio products through immersive, sensory-rich experiences, both online and in-person.

These launches showcased Sonos Roam, Sonos Ray, and Voice Control, introduced during a vibrant “Feel more” event in New York City. For this occasion, we crafted immersive vignettes that replicated the three new colors of the Sonos Roam, and a sound-reactive projection mapping in a stylized living room setup for the Sonos Ray, enhancing the overall sensory experience and engaging attendees on a deeper level.

For the Sonos Era 300 and Era 100, we created an immersive sound experience to highlight their spatial audio capabilities, designing an event that mimicked the aesthetic of a New York City record store. This physical experience was complemented by a digital component and engaging social content.

Additionally, at the CEDIA trade show for two consecutive years, we spotlighted the Sub Mini and Move 2. Every aspect, from spatial design to physical construction, was meticulously crafted to optimize the sound experience within a constructed space. These efforts, spanning over three years, were consistently focused on deepening audience engagement and broadening Sonos's market reach through innovative, multi-sensory marketing strategies.

Oculus

Medal of Honor

Medal of Honor: Above and Beyond, a new installment in the beloved series, is the first to offer a 360° reality gameplay experience, allowing players to step into the shoes of an OSS agent during WWII.


To promote the game's launch and highlight the immersive capabilities of Oculus VR, a unique marketing campaign was developed. This included creating an immersive 3D painting that mimicked the depth of VR on 2D surfaces and used a painterly style inspired by WWII recruitment posters.


The game's interface features paper and pencil textures, reminiscent of mission dossiers from the original Medal of Honor, enhancing the nostalgic feel. The promotional efforts, in collaboration with Meta and EA, aimed to convey the core promise of the game and the unique experiences possible with Oculus hardware.

1/13

East of
the Rockies

Collaborating with Japanese-Canadian author Joy Kogawa, we designed an interactive, AR-powered narrative inspired by her real-life experiences in internment camps.

We rooted the project in extensive research, which informed our historical accuracy and guided our visual style. From there, we crafted an emotional story told from a compelling and immersive first-person perspective.

In our story, 17-year-old Yuki was forcefully displaced from her home to a remote camp in Slocan, BC. Viewers could get to know her family, explore her bunk, and listen to her favourite records.

We developed a massive library of artistically designed 3D assets, including 46 characters and dynamic content, including real-life photographs, newspapers, and personal stories.

1/9

adidas

Unlock the Drop

To help sneakerheads get their hands on coveted Adidas shoes more easily, we created an innovative approach to selling exclusive new releases using an AR drop and automated lockers.


At ComplexCon, a major fashion and pop culture festival in Los Angeles, we created digital countdowns that drummed up anticipation for the release of exclusive new Adidas sneakers.


With each release, people scanned our digital installations, or 'drop spots', to check out the AR shoes. Then, they were directed to an automated locker nearby to snag a pair for themselves.


We sold over 1,000 limited edition adidas in 48 hours. On average, a purchase took 1.5 minutes - 2.5 hours faster than regular purchasing behaviour.

2018

1/17

Nuclear
Dissent

Nuclear Dissent is an engaging interactive documentary that delves into the French Government's nuclear testing in the Pacific Ocean during the 1970s. It narrates the tale through the perspectives of some of the era's most courageous activists and experts, tracing the journey of Greenpeace's founders.


Structured into five captivating chapters, the documentary utilizes WebGL technology to offer a 360° immersive experience, blending intense video and audio narratives to engage the audience fully.


The documentary encourages viewers to delve deeper into the history of nuclear activism, featuring dynamic photo galleries that chronicle the movement's evolution since 1963.


The website's design strikingly juxtaposes the pristine beauty of the Pacific landscapes against the grim aftermath of nuclear testing. The user interface and sound design take inspiration from retro analog interfaces and Geiger counters, enhancing the overall sensory experience.
